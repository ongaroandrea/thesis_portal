## SCOPE

Compare Image A with Image B and infer only the physical camera and lens changes
that transform A into B. Analyze yaw, pitch, roll, truck, dolly, pedestal, and
FOV. Ignore lighting, shadows, materials, textures, color, render quality,
object edits, pose changes, and artistic quality. Perspective change is a camera
cue, not geometry deformation.

The method must adapt to any visible scene: a detailed environment, several
objects, one isolated object, a plain background, a cropped subject, or a weakly
textured image. Use every reliable cue that exists, but never invent a cue that
is absent.

## DIRECTION CONTRACT

Report the physical A-to-B camera or lens operation, not the apparent direction
of pixel motion and not a corrective B-to-A operation.

For a stationary scene:

- look_right and move_right generally shift scene content left on screen
- look_left and move_left generally shift scene content right on screen
- look_up and move_up generally shift scene content down on screen
- look_down and move_down generally shift scene content up on screen
- move_closer or zoom_in makes content larger
- move_farther or zoom_out makes content smaller
- tilt_clockwise rotates stable image structure clockwise
- tilt_counterclockwise rotates it counterclockwise

Screen motion establishes a candidate direction only. Confirm the camera type
from geometry before assigning an operation.

## ADAPTIVE EVIDENCE SELECTION

First identify stable corresponding evidence in both images. Prefer the highest
available tier, and combine tiers when possible:

1. Full-scene evidence: foreground and background features, horizon, vanishing
   directions, repeated lines, object overlaps, floor or wall boundaries.
2. Subject-depth evidence: protruding and recessed parts, front and rear edges,
   openings, corners, visible faces, self-occlusion, and internal feature spacing.
3. Subject-shape evidence: silhouette, bounding box, center, width, height,
   stable axes, recognizable points, and distance from the image center.
4. Composition-only evidence: margins, cropping, and overall displacement.

Do not demand full-scene evidence when subject geometry provides the required
signature. Do not treat a plain background as proof of no change. Conversely,
composition-only evidence is insufficient to distinguish confounded operations.

Before classification, mentally register corresponding points and compare:

- horizontal and vertical displacement
- angular rotation of stable lines or axes
- radial displacement relative to the image center
- local and global scale factors
- face proportions and edge convergence
- overlap, occlusion, and newly visible regions
- whether motion is common across depth or depth-dependent

Ignore correspondences whose appearance changed because of illumination,
reflection, texture, articulation, or geometry edits.

## GEOMETRIC MODELS

Use the model that best explains all reliable observations with the fewest
unsupported changes:

- Rotation changes viewing direction, vanishing geometry, face proportions, or
  top/side visibility, but does not create translational parallax.
- Translation changes viewpoint and can create depth-dependent displacement,
  scale, overlap, or self-occlusion while leaving the camera orientation fixed.
- Roll creates a common image-plane rotation around the view axis.
- FOV creates common radial magnification about the principal point without
  changing viewpoint, overlap, self-occlusion, or face proportions.
- Crop or post-render framing changes composition without providing a physical
  camera signature. Do not force such a change into one of the seven parameters.

## PARAMETER TESTS

### YAW

Positive signatures include changed horizontal vanishing geometry, changed
left/right face proportions, or a consistent turn-in-place pattern across the
scene or subject. A side becoming visible supports yaw only when its edge angles
and face proportions also follow a rotational pattern.

Reject yaw when the only cue is horizontal recentering, cropping, or a common
lateral shift.

### PITCH

Positive signatures include vertical movement of the horizon or vertical
vanishing geometry, changed top-versus-underside visibility, changed upper/lower
face proportions, or a consistent turn-in-place pattern.

Reject pitch when the only cue is vertical recentering or margin change.

### ROLL

Positive signatures include a common clockwise or counterclockwise rotation of
multiple stable scene lines, subject axes, or non-parallel edges. Distinguish a
global rotation from one naturally slanted or curved feature.

### TRUCK

Positive signatures include depth-dependent lateral parallax, changed lateral
overlap, or different horizontal displacement of protruding/recessed or
foreground/background parts while vanishing directions remain stable.

Reject truck when all usable content undergoes only one uniform horizontal
shift and no viewpoint signature is visible.

### DOLLY

Positive signatures include unequal scale change across depth, changed
perspective strength, radial displacement that is stronger for nearer parts, or
changed overlap/self-occlusion caused by camera distance.

Reject dolly when the only cue is overall image or subject size.

### PEDESTAL

Positive signatures include depth-dependent vertical parallax, changed vertical
overlap, or different vertical displacement of protruding/recessed or
foreground/background parts while orientation cues remain stable.

Reject pedestal when all usable content undergoes only one uniform vertical
shift and no viewpoint signature is visible.

### FOV

Positive signatures include one common radial scale factor across reliable
features at different positions or depths, with unchanged face proportions,
perspective, overlap, visibility, and self-occlusion.

Reject FOV when scale ratios vary with depth or viewpoint-dependent visibility
changes.

## REQUIRED CONFOUNDER TESTS

### YAW VS TRUCK

- Choose yaw for changed vanishing/edge directions or rotational face changes
  without lateral parallax.
- Choose truck for lateral parallax or overlap changes with stable orientation.
- If only a uniform lateral frame shift is visible, set both to none.
- Report both only if independent rotation and translation residuals remain.

### PITCH VS PEDESTAL

- Choose pitch for changed horizon/edge directions or rotational top/underside
  visibility without vertical parallax.
- Choose pedestal for vertical parallax or overlap changes with stable
  orientation.
- If only a uniform vertical frame shift is visible, set both to none.
- Report both only if independent rotation and translation residuals remain.

### DOLLY VS FOV

- Choose dolly for unequal depth scaling, perspective change, or changed
  overlap/self-occlusion.
- Choose FOV for a common radial scale with unchanged viewpoint relationships.
- If only one flat region or an object without usable depth structure changes
  size, set both to none because the cause is not identifiable.
- Report both only if a depth-dependent distance signature and an additional
  common radial scale are separately supported.

## MIXED-CHANGE ANALYSIS

The pair may contain zero, one, or several camera changes. Do not assume a fixed
count. When several changes seem possible:

1. Identify the strongest global pattern.
2. Explain the image with that pattern.
3. Inspect only the residual mismatch for another parameter.
4. Add another parameter only when the residual has its own named geometric
   signature.

Do not assign two confounded parameters to the same pixels. A single cue cannot
serve as independent proof of multiple changes.

## DIRECTION CHECKS

After selecting a parameter, convert observed image motion to the physical
operation:

- content left plus yaw evidence -> look_right
- content right plus yaw evidence -> look_left
- content left plus truck evidence -> move_right
- content right plus truck evidence -> move_left
- content down plus pitch evidence -> look_up
- content up plus pitch evidence -> look_down
- content down plus pedestal evidence -> move_up
- content up plus pedestal evidence -> move_down
- larger content plus dolly evidence -> move_closer
- smaller content plus dolly evidence -> move_farther
- larger content plus uniform FOV evidence -> zoom_in
- smaller content plus uniform FOV evidence -> zoom_out

For roll, report the observed common image-plane rotation direction. For mixed
motions, use the component-specific geometric signature rather than the net
motion of the subject center.

## UNCERTAINTY, CONFIDENCE, AND EVIDENCE

- Every non-none result must name a visible region, feature, contour, edge, or
  relationship and state the parameter-specific signature found there.
- High confidence requires at least two independent supporting cues.
- Medium confidence requires one clear diagnostic cue.
- Low confidence may describe a weak result, but compatibility alone is not
  enough for a non-none operation.
- Use operation none and magnitude none when evidence is absent, too weak, or
  cannot distinguish confounded explanations.
- Use indeterminate only when the parameter definitely changed but its physical
  direction cannot be resolved.
- Missing background structure lowers the available evidence; it does not lower
  confidence when subject geometry independently supplies strong evidence.
- Do not invent a horizon, vanishing point, depth layer, or correspondence.

## MAGNITUDE

Assign small, medium, or high only after detecting a change. Use the supplied
dataset magnitude definitions when present. Otherwise judge magnitude from the
strength of the relevant geometric effect, not merely from total pixel motion.
Every none operation must have magnitude none.

## FINAL SELF-CONSISTENCY PASS

For each of the seven parameters, verify:

1. Is there a visible parameter-specific signature?
2. Does it distinguish the parameter from its main confounder?
3. Is the reported direction the physical A-to-B operation?
4. Is the evidence unaffected by non-camera changes?
5. If multiple parameters are reported, does each have independent evidence?

If any required check fails, change that parameter to none with magnitude none
and briefly state which diagnostic signature was unavailable. Keep all evidence
short, concrete, and tied to visible image regions.
