## ABSOLUTE EXCLUSIONS

Analyze only camera and framing. Never report lighting, shadows, materials,
textures, render quality, object changes, geometry changes, or artistic quality.
Perspective differences are not geometry deformation.

This prompt is intended for scenes with a plain or featureless background. Base
the comparison on the visible subject: its silhouette, stable internal features,
recognizable edges, face proportions, overlaps, apparent scale, and position
relative to the image center and frame boundaries. Do not require background
detail, a horizon, or multiple scene objects.

## DIRECTION CONTRACT — READ FIRST

Every operation describes the physical camera/lens change that transforms
Image A into Image B. It does NOT describe how to correct Image B back to
Image A, and it does NOT describe the direction in which the subject moves on
the screen.

For a stationary subject, camera motion and apparent image motion are opposite:

- camera looks right -> the subject moves left in Image B
- camera looks left -> the subject moves right in Image B
- camera trucks right -> the subject moves left in Image B
- camera trucks left -> the subject moves right in Image B
- camera looks up -> the subject moves down in Image B
- camera looks down -> the subject moves up in Image B
- camera moves up -> the subject moves down; camera moves down produces the
  opposite result
- camera moves closer or narrows the FOV -> the subject expands away from the
  principal point
- camera moves farther or widens the FOV -> the subject contracts toward the
  principal point

Infer the physical A-to-B camera operation first, then emit its label. Never
name an operation from screen-space motion alone.

## PRE-ANALYSIS REGISTRATION

Before classifying anything, align the two images conceptually using the same
stable parts of the subject. Compare:

- the subject bounding box, center, width, and height
- two or more recognizable points on different parts of the subject
- the outer contour and any straight or curved edges
- visible face proportions and top-versus-side visibility
- internal overlap, self-occlusion, and newly revealed or hidden regions
- each measured point's direction and distance from the image center

Ignore changes caused only by lighting, reflections, material appearance, or
other non-camera effects. If the subject itself changes pose or geometry, do
not reinterpret that change as camera motion.

Use these invariants:

- Translation changes viewpoint and may alter self-occlusion, relative feature
  spacing, and perspective across the subject.
- Rotation changes viewing direction and the proportions or directions of
  visible faces and edges.
- FOV changes image magnification radially about the principal point without
  changing viewpoint, face visibility, or self-occlusion.
- A pure crop or uniform frame shift changes composition but does not by itself
  identify a physical camera parameter.

## PARAMETER TESTS

### 1. YAW

Positive evidence:
- left-versus-right face proportions change
- the directions or convergence of subject edges change consistently
- a side becomes more or less visible as expected from a turn in place
- the change is rotational rather than a uniform horizontal displacement

Do not infer yaw from subject recentering or unequal side margins alone.

### 2. PITCH

Positive evidence:
- top-versus-underside visibility changes consistently
- vertical face proportions or the convergence of subject edges change
- upper and lower subject features move in a pattern consistent with a turn in
  place rather than a uniform vertical displacement

Do not infer pitch from a vertical frame shift alone.

### 3. ROLL

Positive evidence:
- stable subject axes or edges acquire one consistent angular rotation
- several non-parallel parts of the subject rotate by the same image-plane
  angle

Do not infer roll from one locally curved or slanted edge.

### 4. TRUCK

Positive evidence:
- horizontal viewpoint change alters self-occlusion or relative spacing across
  the subject while its edge directions remain broadly consistent
- protruding and recessed parts shift by different horizontal amounts
- one side is revealed through lateral parallax rather than a turn in place

Do not infer truck from a uniform horizontal shift, subject recentering, or
margin change alone.

### 5. DOLLY

Positive evidence:
- camera distance changes produce perspective change across the subject
- protruding and recessed parts change scale or separation by different
  factors
- self-occlusion or the relative size of near and far subject parts changes

Subject size change alone is not dolly evidence.

### 6. PEDESTAL

Positive evidence:
- vertical viewpoint change alters self-occlusion or relative spacing between
  upper and lower parts while edge directions remain broadly consistent
- top or underside regions are revealed through vertical parallax rather than
  a turn in place

Do not infer pedestal from a uniform vertical shift or margin change alone.

### 7. FOV

Positive evidence:
- the subject contour and its internal features share approximately one radial
  scale factor about the same principal point
- face proportions, self-occlusion, and visible regions remain unchanged
- corresponding points move toward or away from the image center in proportion
  to their original radius

Do not use a scale change with altered perspective or self-occlusion as FOV
evidence.

## MANDATORY PAIRWISE GATES

### YAW VS TRUCK

1. If face proportions or edge directions change as a rotation signature and
   lateral parallax is absent, choose yaw and set truck to none.
2. If edge directions remain stable while self-occlusion or relative feature
   spacing changes laterally, choose truck and set yaw to none.
3. If the only cue is uniform horizontal displacement, set both to none.
4. Report both only when separate rotation and translation signatures remain
   after accounting for each other.

### PITCH VS PEDESTAL

1. If top/underside visibility and edge directions change as a rotation
   signature without vertical parallax, choose pitch and set pedestal to none.
2. If edge directions remain stable while self-occlusion or relative feature
   spacing changes vertically, choose pedestal and set pitch to none.
3. If the only cue is uniform vertical displacement, set both to none.
4. Report both only when separate rotation and translation signatures are
   visible.

### DOLLY VS FOV

1. If self-occlusion, perspective, or scale ratios between protruding and
   recessed subject parts change, choose dolly and set FOV to none unless an
   additional uniform radial scale remains.
2. If the entire subject shares one radial scale and its perspective and
   visibility relationships stay unchanged, choose FOV and set dolly to none.
3. If only overall subject size changes and the subject provides no usable
   depth structure, set both to none because the cause is not identifiable.
4. Report both only when a depth-dependent distance signature and a separate
   common radial scale are independently visible.

## PLAIN-BACKGROUND FALLBACK

A featureless background is missing evidence, not evidence that a parameter is
unchanged. Use the subject-only tests above. When the available subject does
not contain enough visible geometry to separate two explanations, return none
for the confounded parameters and state exactly which signature is unavailable.
Do not invent a horizon, background point, floor edge, or second depth layer.

## EVIDENCE AND CONFIDENCE RULES

- Each non-none parameter needs a named visible subject feature and a
  parameter-specific cue.
- Describe evidence using concrete regions such as "left rim," "top face,"
  "front tip," "rear opening," or "outer silhouette."
- Do not reuse one generic cue as evidence for two confounded parameters.
- High confidence requires two independent cues. Medium requires one clear cue.
- When evidence is weak or merely compatible with a change, use none.
- Use indeterminate only when a camera change is unmistakable but its direction
  cannot be determined; do not use it for ordinary uncertainty.
- The number of true changes may be zero, one, or several. Do not add a second
  parameter merely because it can also explain the same pixels.

## FINAL SELF-CONSISTENCY PASS

For every non-none output, verify all three conditions:

1. The evidence names a visible subject feature, contour, or edge.
2. The evidence distinguishes this parameter from its confounder.
3. The operation direction is the physical A-to-B camera/lens direction after
   applying the direction contract, not the observed screen-motion direction.

If any condition fails, change that parameter to none. For every none result,
use magnitude none and briefly state which required signature was absent.
