HDRI / EXR / ENVIRONMENT / GLOBAL ILLUMINATION RULES

Use ONLY this file's logic for identifying and comparing environment lighting.
Do not put HDRI/EXR-specific reasoning in the general light prompt or the
general parameter explanation.

If the annotation or prompt context says an HDRI or EXR environment is present,
still verify its visible effect from the images. Do not infer the actual file,
map content, renderer setting, or hidden lighting rig.

Environment lighting and direct lights can coexist. Evaluate the environment
contribution separately from every direct light source.

================================================================
1. IDENTIFYING ENVIRONMENT/HDRI-LIKE LIGHTING
================================================================

Treat Image A as environment/HDRI-lit if it shows:
- No single dominant cast shadow direction
- Soft, low-contrast ambient illumination visible on most surfaces
- Objects lit from multiple sides at once, with varying intensity per side
- Illumination color that varies gradually by facing direction (e.g. sky-facing
  surfaces cooler/bluer, ground-facing surfaces warmer)
- Reflective surfaces showing broad panoramic content rather than a single
  compact highlight

If a clear single-direction cast shadow with a hard or moderately soft edge
IS present, treat that as a direct light source (directional/area/spot/point)
even if ambient fill is also visible — environment lighting and a direct
light can coexist. Only the ambient/fill portion of the image should be
judged using this file; the direct light gets evaluated separately.

In Step 0 light counting, environment/HDRI/EXR illumination may be represented
as one global environment contribution only when it has visible image evidence.
Do not count bounce light, reflections, or indirect illumination as separate
lights unless they reveal a visible emissive source.

================================================================
2. VALID EVIDENCE — COMPARE ONLY THESE BETWEEN IMAGE A AND IMAGE B
================================================================

- Overall ambient/fill light level (how bright shadowed and grazing-angle
  surfaces appear)
- Directional bias of the ambient fill (e.g. noticeably brighter from one
  side even without a hard shadow)
- Softness/hardness of any shadows present — environment lighting alone
  produces very soft or absent shadows; if shadows sharpened, that signals
  a change toward a more direct/dominant light, not just an environment change
- Shadow fill (how dark shadowed regions are, independent of the direct
  light's own intensity)
- Illumination color temperature of the ambient fill specifically (not the
  whole image — a warm ambient fill with a cool direct light is normal)
- Reflection/highlight content used ONLY as evidence of environment change
  (e.g. reflected content looks flatter, dimmer, or differently colored) —
  never as evidence of a material or reflectivity change
- Bounce-light visibility (soft secondary illumination on nearby surfaces
  facing away from any direct light)
- Fresnel/rim brightening ONLY when it is corroborated by ambient fill,
  grazing-angle illumination, reflection content, or environment color evidence

================================================================
3. INVALID EVIDENCE — NEVER USE
================================================================

- Do NOT count bounce light or reflections as independent light sources
- Do NOT treat a change in surface albedo/roughness as an environment
  lighting change, even if it affects how much of the environment reflects
  off the surface
- Do NOT interpret exposure/post-processing brightness shifts (that scale
  both lit and shadowed regions equally) as an ambient fill change — ambient
  fill changes affect shadowed/grazing regions disproportionately more than
  directly-lit regions
- Do NOT assume environment lighting is present just because shadows are
  soft — soft shadows can also come from a large/close area light; only
  call it environment lighting if there is no single dominant shadow
  direction at all (see Section 1)
- Do NOT use Fresnel/rim brightening alone to infer an HDRI/EXR change.
- Do NOT interpret IOR, specular, roughness, metallic, or albedo changes as
  environment-lighting changes.

================================================================
4. HOW TO REPORT AN ENVIRONMENT/HDRI/EXR DIFFERENCE
================================================================

When an environment contribution is visible, fill the dedicated
`analysis.environment_lighting` object in the JSON output.

Use only these status mappings:

- Ambient/environment intensity got stronger or weaker overall
  -> environment_lighting.ambient_intensity.status: stronger | weaker
  -> environment_lighting.overall_status: stronger | weaker

- Ambient/environment direction or HDRI placement shifted
  -> environment_lighting.ambient_direction.status: shifted | rotated
  -> environment_lighting.reflection_environment.status: shifted | rotated
  -> environment_lighting.overall_status: rotated

- Shadow fill or shadow softness changed as a result of the ambient
  contribution changing (not a direct light's shadow)
  -> environment_lighting.shadow_fill.status: stronger | weaker
  -> light.shadow_behavior.status may also be harder | softer when direct
     shadow evidence supports it

- Ambient color temperature shifted
  -> environment_lighting.ambient_color.status: warmer | cooler
  -> environment_lighting.overall_status: warmer | cooler

- Reflected/panoramic environment content changed
  -> environment_lighting.reflection_environment.status: changed
  -> environment_lighting.overall_status: changed

If no environment contribution is visible, set:
- present_in_image_a: no
- present_in_image_b: no
- overall_status: same
- all child statuses: same

================================================================
5. QUICK-REFERENCE
================================================================

Signal observed in Image B vs Image A             JSON field to fill
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Shadowed areas noticeably brighter/darker          environment_lighting.ambient_intensity.status:
                                                   stronger | weaker
Reflections show flatter/dimmer environment        environment_lighting.reflection_environment.status:
                                                   changed | weaker
Reflected environment appears shifted/rotated      environment_lighting.reflection_environment.status:
                                                   shifted | rotated
Shadow fill changed due to ambient contribution    environment_lighting.shadow_fill.status:
                                                   stronger | weaker
Ambient hue shifted at similar brightness          environment_lighting.ambient_color.status:
                                                   warmer | cooler

================================================================
6. FRESNEL / RIM EVIDENCE FOR HDRI OR EXR
================================================================

Fresnel/rim brightening may be used only as environment-lighting evidence
when it is corroborated by ambient fill, grazing-angle illumination,
reflection content, or environment color behavior.

Valid uses:
- Rim brightness changes together with ambient fill.
- Grazing-angle surfaces receive more or less environment light.
- Reflective surfaces show environment content that is dimmer, flatter,
  warmer, cooler, or visibly different.

Invalid uses:
- Do not report material IOR, roughness, specular, metallic, texture, or
  albedo changes.
- Do not use Fresnel/rim evidence alone to infer light type changes.
